## Motivation

Conventional liquid-metal (EGaIn) inks suffer from three intertwined problems: they require **mechanical or laser sintering** to become conductive, they need **adhesion primers** for stable bonding to soft substrates, and most reported formulations rely on **organic solvents** that complicate patterning on skin or biocompatible polymers.

## Approach

I am developing **PDA-LM**, a sintering-free, primer-free, water-based liquid-metal ink in which **polydopamine (PDA) chelates the native amorphous Ga₂O₃ shell of EGaIn particles** through catechol–Ga³⁺ coordination. The coordination simultaneously (i) bridges particle–particle contacts so the ink becomes conductive without post-sintering, and (ii) introduces hydroxyl-rich adhesive linkages to a wide range of substrates (PDMS, PVA, paper, skin).

## Evidence

The chelation is supported by:

- **UV-Vis & ITC** following the Holten-Andersen pH-stoichiometry framework for catechol–Ga³⁺ coordination
- **XPS** Ga 3d / N 1s correlations on the cured film
- **DFT (ORCA r2SCAN-3c, wB97X-D3/TZVP with BSSE)** of -OH-terminated amorphous Ga₂O₃ surfaces interacting with catechol fragments
