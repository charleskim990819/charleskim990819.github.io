### Research interests

<div class="ll-grid-2">
<div class="ll-tile">
<span class="ll-tile-kicker">Materials</span>
<h4>Stretchable conductors</h4>
<p>Catechol–Ga³⁺ coordination chemistry for liquid-metal inks that adhere and percolate without sintering or primer; water-transfer printing of those inks onto skin and elastomer; DFT and MD studies of the gallium-oxide surface that makes all of it difficult.</p>
</div>
<div class="ll-tile">
<span class="ll-tile-kicker">Electrochemistry</span>
<h4>Printed electrochemical sensors</h4>
<p>Screen-printed three-electrode cells on elastomer substrates, solid-contact Na⁺ ion-selective electrodes, and diffusion-limiting membranes for selectivity. Device-to-device spread is treated as a result in its own right, not as an error bar to be minimised in the caption.</p>
</div>
<div class="ll-tile">
<span class="ll-tile-kicker">Instrumentation</span>
<h4>Wearable instrumentation</h4>
<p>Four-channel AD5941 potentiostats on flexible PCB with BLE telemetry and a companion phone app; multimodal bands that add bio-impedance, PPG and inertial channels; ear-EEG front ends for non-invasive BCI.</p>
</div>
<div class="ll-tile">
<span class="ll-tile-kicker">Biointerfaces</span>
<h4>Tissue-facing coatings</h4>
<p>PDRN-based regenerative coatings and anti-fouling lubricant chemistry for surgical sutures and intramuscular EMG electrodes, judged by what happens to the electrode–tissue impedance over weeks.</p>
</div>
</div>

### Current work

Roughly in order of how much bench time each one gets.

<div class="ll-threads">
<div class="ll-thread">
<div class="ll-thread-n">01</div>
<div class="ll-thread-body">
<h4>PDA-LM ink</h4>
<p>Catechol–Ga³⁺ coordination arrests the native gallium-oxide skin on EGaIn rather than stripping it, which lets a water-based ink print, stick and conduct in a single step. The open question is no longer the day-one resistance but how that interface behaves under sustained electrochemical load, followed operando instead of inferred from before-and-after images.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="ll-thread">
<div class="ll-thread-n">02</div>
<div class="ll-thread-body">
<h4>Wearable potentiostat toolkit</h4>
<p>A four-channel AD5941 and ESP32-C6 front end running cyclic voltammetry, chronoamperometry, impedance spectroscopy and open-circuit potentiometry — now on flexible PCB, over BLE, with a Flutter app on the other end. A benchtop potentiostat cannot follow an electrode onto a moving body; this is the instrument that can.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="ll-thread">
<div class="ll-thread-n">03</div>
<div class="ll-thread-body">
<h4>Printed electrodes, honestly characterised</h4>
<p>Screen-printed carbon and Ag/AgCl on elastomer, measured across tens of nominally identical devices. Yield and device-to-device spread are reported as the primary numbers, since those are what decide whether a soft electrode survives outside a demo.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="ll-thread">
<div class="ll-thread-n">04</div>
<div class="ll-thread-body">
<h4>PDRN and lubricant coatings</h4>
<p>A dual coating meant to suppress fouling and support tissue regeneration at the same time, on sutures and intramuscular EMG electrodes. Impedance spectroscopy at the tissue interface is the endpoint that actually separates the groups; the EMG amplitudes establish non-inferiority and little more.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

Longer write-ups for each are on the [projects](/projects/) page.
